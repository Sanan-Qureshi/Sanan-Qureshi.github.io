SCIENCE · EDUCATION · LEADERSHIP

Find What I Do at The Black Hole

Beyond teaching, I have had the opportunity to introduce initiatives, design experiments, and develop educational programmes to make science more accessible and engaging.

01 · EXPERIMENTAL SCIENCE

Designing New Experiments

We designed many experiments at TBH Science Lab. One example is Rubin's Tube, which is used to visualise standing waves and observe how their behaviour changes with frequency.

Watch the experiment →
02 · STUDENT RESEARCH

Introducing Science Internship Programme

In 2025, I proposed introducing this internship programme for high-school students so that they could learn how research is actually conducted. Students work in teams, study the theory behind a specific project, and then perform experiments to investigate whether the theory works.

They vary different parameters, investigate trends, and learn how to extrapolate scientific results.

03 · ASTRONOMY EDUCATION

Introducing Free Astronomy and Astrophysics Lecture Series

In January 2026, I took another initiative at TBH. Thanks to Dr Saeeda for agreeing to deliver the lecture series, we created an opportunity for peers to learn astronomy and astrophysics for free.

COURSE CURRICULUM DESIGNER · STEM INSTRUCTOR

What I Do at Polymath-Kids

I have designed two course curricula and taught skill-oriented STEM courses for young learners.

01 · Course Curriculum Designer

Astronomy and Experimental Science

I’ve created a two-level Astronomy course—Beginner and Advanced—built around hands-on, interactive activities that make exploring the cosmos fun and accessible. Building on that approach, I also recently designed an Experimental Science course where students get practical, hands-on experience in Physics, Biology, and Chemistry using everyday household items.

02 · STEM Instructor

Coding, Data & AI for Young Learners

At Polymath-Kids, I also teach skill-oriented courses in Python, C++, Data Science, and Machine Learning for Kids. It is truly inspiring to see young learners aged 12 to 15 bring so much passion and energy to these subjects—and the best part is fielding their endless stream of curious questions, which constantly pushes me to think outside the box as an educator.
